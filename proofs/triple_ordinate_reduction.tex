\begin{abstract}
We give an exact transfer identity from the existing weighted full-zero
theorem, and prove that its two real-test kernel errors vanish.
The remaining complex-argument error is isolated, with a sufficient
positive-moment bound. Eight independent reflections isolate an even
hyperbolic term; the remaining kernel-mixing error vanishes on a smaller
fixed support region. On that region its even kernel can also be replaced
by the constant $3\pi/8$ with a vanishing error. The resulting signed term
is still open: this draft does not prove
an ordinate-only correlation asymptotic.
\end{abstract}

triples in $I^3$. Equal indices, equal complex zeros at different indices,

Fix nonnegative $\omega\in C_c^\infty((1,2))$ of integral one, extended
by zero. For $T\geq2\pi e$ put
\[
 q=\log T,\qquad b=\log(T/(2\pi)),\qquad \ell=\frac b{2\pi},
 \qquad c_T=\frac{16}{3Tq}.
\]
Fix $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\R^2)$ supported in
$h(\xi,\eta)=\max(|\xi|,|\eta|,|\xi+\eta|)\leq s=1-\kappa<1$.
Use $\widehat F(\xi,\eta)=\int F(u,v)e^{-2\pi i(u\xi+v\eta)}\dd u\dd v$
and the entire extension
\[
 F(z_1,z_2)=\int_{\R^2}\phi(\xi,\eta)
              e^{2\pi i(\xi z_1+\eta z_2)}\dd\xi\dd\eta.
\]
For a triple, abbreviate
\[
 a=\gamma_{i_2}-\gamma_{i_1},\quad d=\gamma_{i_3}-\gamma_{i_1},\quad
 (u,v)=\ell(a,d),\quad r=\max(|u|,|v|),
\]
\[
 z=(u-i\ell(\delta_{i_2}+\delta_{i_1}),
       v-i\ell(\delta_{i_3}+\delta_{i_1})),\qquad
 w=\omega(\gamma_{i_1}/T).
\]
The conventional all-ordered ordinate statistic is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ordinate}
 O_T[F]=\frac1{T\ell}\sum_{I^3}wF(u,v).
\end{equation}
Only the anchor is localized. No simplicity, ordinate-distinctness,
sharp cutoff, or exclusion of partial diagonals is imposed.

Write $J_\delta=J(a,d;\delta_{i_2},\delta_{i_3},\delta_{i_1})$,
$J_0=J(a,d;0,0,0)$, and $J_*=3\pi/8$. Explicitly, with
$c_1=a-i\delta_{i_2}$, $c_2=d-i\delta_{i_3}$, $c_3=i\delta_{i_1}$,
and $d_{mn}=c_m-c_n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:profile}
 J_\delta=\pi\frac{24+d_{12}^2+d_{13}^2+d_{23}^2}
 {(4+d_{12}^2)(4+d_{13}^2)(4+d_{23}^2)}
 =\int_\R\prod_{m=1}^3\frac1{1+(t-c_m)^2}\dd t.
\end{equation}
The profile and its integral representation are
\texttt{TRIPLE-KERNEL-PROFILE-001}. The baseline theorem
\texttt{TRIPLE-SMOOTHED-CORRELATION-001} states that
$R_T^J=c_T\sum wJ_\delta F(z)=S_3[F]+o(1)$ for fixed data.
Here, with $s_0(t)=\sin(\pi t)/(\pi t)$ and $s_0(0)=1$,
\begin{align*}
 R_2(t)&=1-s_0(t)^2,\\
 R_3(u,v)&=\det\begin{pmatrix}1&s_0(u)&s_0(v)\\s_0(u)&1&s_0(u-v)\\
 s_0(v)&s_0(u-v)&1\end{pmatrix},\\
 S_3[F]&=\int_{\R^2}FR_3\dd u\dd v+
 \int_\R R_2(t)\{F(0,t)+F(t,0)+F(t,t)\}\dd t+F(0,0).
\end{align*}
Pairings are bilinear; $F$ need not be real or symmetric.

\section{Exact transfer and convergence}
\begin{lemma}[Exact reduction; \texttt{ORDINATE-TRANSFER-001}]
\label{lem:transfer}
Define signed errors, without taking absolute values inside the sums, by
\begin{align}
 \Ea&=c_T\sum wJ_\delta\{F(z)-F(u,v)\},\label{eq:arguments}\\
 \Eh&=c_T\sum w(J_\delta-J_0)F(u,v),\label{eq:horizontal}\\
 \Eg&=c_T\sum w(J_0-J_*)F(u,v).\label{eq:gaps}
\end{align}
All these sums converge absolutely at each fixed $T$. Exactly,
\begin{align}
 R_T^J&=\frac bq O_T+\Ea+\Eh+\Eg,\label{eq:transfer}\\
 O_T-S_3[F]&=\frac qb\{R_T^J-S_3[F]-\Ea-\Eh-\Eg\}
                   +\left(\frac qb-1\right)S_3[F].\label{eq:main-transfer}
\end{align}
Zero cutoffs may tend independently to infinity at fixed $T$ and fixed
test; only afterwards is $T$ allowed to grow.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For real $x$ and $|e|\leq1/2$,
$|1+(x-ie)^2|\geq1+x^2-e^2\geq\tfrac34(1+x^2)$.
The first two $e$ derivatives of its reciprocal are bounded by
$C(1+x^2)^{-1}$: differentiate the rational function and use this
inequality, with $|x-ie|\ll(1+x^2)^{1/2}$.
Consequently $J$ and its horizontal derivatives through order two are
uniformly bounded for all real gaps and the closed parameter cube
$[-1/2,1/2]^3$. Indeed, bound two real kernels by one and integrate the
third. Differentiation under the integral is justified locally in the
parameters by an integrable dominating function; the resulting bounds
are global. Colliding centers cause no exception.

The anchor has finitely many occurrences. The unit-interval count
\texttt{PAIR-COUNT-001} gives $O(\log(|t|+2))$ zeros in a unit interval
near $t$, with multiplicity. At fixed $T$ the imaginary coordinates
of $z$ are bounded by $\ell$. Integrating the compact smooth Fourier
integral by parts gives, for every $N$, a bound
$|F(z)|+|F(u,v)|\leq C_{T,N,\phi}(1+r)^{-N}$.
Two unit-interval summations therefore converge absolutely. This
also justifies reindexing by horizontal reflection and all cutoff
limits here. Telescope each summand. Finally
$c_TJ_*=2\pi/(Tq)=(b/q)/(T\ell)$, proving both identities.
\end{proof}

\section{Two unconditional counting inputs}
These are imported source statements, not numerical project certificates.
Their upstream analytic proofs have not been reconstructed in this extension.

\begin{proposition}[Close-pair input; \texttt{ORDINATE-PAIR-INPUT-001}]
\label{prop:pair-input}
For $V$ sufficiently large, $L_V=\log V/(2\pi)$ and
$1\leq\lambda\leq\sqrt{L_V}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair-input}
 \sum_{\substack{0<\gamma,\gamma'\leq V\\
                  |\gamma-\gamma'|L_V\leq\lambda}}
 \left(1-\frac{|\gamma-\gamma'|L_V}{\lambda}\right)
 \ll V\log V\,\lambda.
\end{equation}
The sum includes all ordered occurrences, including equal ordinates.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Source and deduction]
GLSS~\cite{GLSS} (\textbf{PREPRINT}), equation (6.1), gives the left side as
$VL_V\{\lambda+\log(2+\lambda)/(\pi^2\lambda)
+O(\sqrt{\log(2+\lambda)}/\lambda)\}$ uniformly for
$0<\lambda^2\leq L_V$. For $\lambda\geq1$ each displayed term is
$O(\lambda)$. Their Section 6 derives this from the unconditional
Propositions 1--2; their PCC-dependent main theorem and the RH improvement
in Remark 2 are not used. No explicit constant is asserted here.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Density input; \texttt{ORDINATE-DENSITY-INPUT-001}]
\label{prop:density-input}
Let $N(\sigma,V)$ count $\beta>\sigma$, $0<\gamma\leq V$, with
multiplicity. Uniformly for $0\leq D\leq0.331$ and
$V\geq H_0=3.0610046\cdot10^{10}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:density-input}
 N(1/2+D,2V)-N(1/2+D,V)
 \ll V^{1-D/4}\log V+(\log V)^2.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Source and deduction]
Simoni\v c, Theorem 1, consulted in arXiv:1910.08274v2, p.~2
(published in J.~Math.~Anal.~Appl.~491 (2020), 124303), bounds this
difference by
\[
10395.2 V^{1-D/4}\log V+1.104\log^2V+
0.173\log V\log\log V+0.51\log V.
\]
This implies \eqref{eq:density-input} with an effective absolute constant.
The consulted author text is distinguished from the journal version.
Its external finite-height and numerical inputs are inherited, not replayed.
No zero in our sums is replaced by a critical-line zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Triple counts; \texttt{ORDINATE-COUNT-001}]
\label{lem:counts}
Let $H_T(R)$ count all triples with $T\leq\gamma_{i_1}\leq2T$
and $r\leq R$. For sufficiently large $T$,
\begin{align}
 H_T(R)&\ll Tq^2R^2 &&(1\leq R\leq R_0=q^{1/4}),\label{eq:small-count}\\
 H_T(R)&\ll Tq^3(1+R)^4 &&(R\geq1).\label{eq:global-count}
\end{align}
Put $A_{20}(F)=\sup_{u,v}(1+\max(|u|,|v|))^{20}|F(u,v)|$ and
$W=\|\omega\|_\infty$. Then
\begin{align}
 \frac1{Tq}\sum_{r\leq R_0}w|F(u,v)|&\ll WA_{20}(F)q,\label{eq:mass}\\
 \frac1{Tq}\sum_{r>R_0}w|F(u,v)|&\ll WA_{20}(F)q^{-2}.\label{eq:tail}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the small range $R/\ell<1$. All participating zeros have positive
ordinates at most $3T$, and each anchor has $O(q)$ possible partners.
Apply \eqref{eq:pair-input} at $V=3T$ and
$\lambda=2RL_{3T}/\ell$. For large $T$ this belongs to
$[1,\sqrt{L_{3T}}]$ throughout $R\leq R_0$. Every pair at distance
at most $R/\ell$ has triangular weight at least $1/2$.
Thus there are $O(TqR)$ anchor--partner pairs. Multiplying by the
$O(q)$ choices for the third occurrence proves even $O(Tq^2R)$,
and hence \eqref{eq:small-count}.

Globally there are $O(Tq)$ anchors and at most
$C(1+R/\ell)\log(T+R/\ell+2)$ partners per anchor, by unit counts
and conjugation for negative ordinates. Since $\ell\geq1$ eventually,
this is at most $Cq(1+R)^2$, giving \eqref{eq:global-count}.
For \eqref{eq:mass}, use $r\leq1$ and dyadic shells up to $R_0$;
their normalized bounds are $CWA_{20}q\sum_{k\geq0}2^{(2-20)k}$.
A shell cut by $R_0$ uses $H_T(R_0)$ and has the same bound.
For the tail use shells $2^kR_0<r\leq2^{k+1}R_0$ and
\eqref{eq:global-count}; the result is
$CWA_{20}q^2R_0^{4-20}=CWA_{20}q^{-2}$.
\end{proof}

\section{Removing the real-test kernel}
\begin{theorem}[Real-test errors; \texttt{ORDINATE-REAL-KERNEL-001}]
\label{thm:real-kernel}
For every Schwartz $F$ on $\R^2$ and the smoothing above, as $T\to\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kernel-errors}
 |\Eg|\ll \frac{WA_{20}(F)}q,\qquad
 |\Eh|\ll WA_{20}(F)\frac{(\log q)^2}q.
\end{equation}
Constants and the lower threshold for $T$ are independent of $F,\omega$.
No Fourier support is needed for these two estimates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The rational profile is smooth near $(a,d)=(0,0)$, is even under
$(a,d)\mapsto(-a,-d)$, and equals $J_*$ at the origin. Together
with its global bound this gives
$|J_0-J_*|\leq C\min(a^2+d^2,1)$.
On $r\leq R_0$ use $C r^2/\ell^2$ and the same dyadic argument as
\eqref{eq:mass}: the bound is
\[
CWA_{20}q\ell^{-2}\sum_{k\geq0}2^{(4-20)k}\ll WA_{20}/q.
\]
On the tail use \eqref{eq:tail}.

For the horizontal error, reflect all three indices simultaneously by
$\rho\mapsto1-\bar\rho$. This preserves ordinates, multiplicities,
$w$, $F(u,v)$ and every index coincidence pattern, while sending
$\delta\mapsto-\delta$. Hence in \eqref{eq:horizontal} one may
replace $J_\delta-J_0$ by
$\tfrac12(J_\delta+J_{-\delta})-J_0$.
Taylor's formula and the uniform second-derivative bounds in the proof
of Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer} give
\[
 \left|\tfrac12(J_\delta+J_{-\delta})-J_0\right|
 \leq C\max_j|\delta_{i_j}|^2.
\]
This is a reindexing of the sum, not a termwise positivity assertion.

Take $D=64\log q/q$, eventually in $(0,0.331)$.
On $r\leq R_0$ with every $|\delta_{i_j}|\leq D$, equation
\eqref{eq:mass} bounds the error by $CWA_{20}qD^2$.
For the remaining tuples some occurrence with $|\delta|>D$ lies in
$[T-1,2T+1]$. Reflection and \eqref{eq:density-input}, applied to the
three windows $(T/2,T],(T,2T],(2T,4T]$, show that their number is
\[
 B_T(D)\ll Tq\exp\{-D\log(T/2)/4\}+q^2
          \ll Tq^{-7}+q^2.
\]
The last inequality uses $\log(T/2)/q\geq1/2$ and thus an exponent
at most $-8\log q$. Each such occurrence belongs to at most $Cq^2$
of our near tuples: fix its slot, and use two unit-interval counts
within distance two. Union over the three slots only increases this
bound. Boundedness of the kernel and $|F|\leq A_{20}$ give a normalized
contribution $CWA_{20}(q^{-6}+q^3/T)$.
The tail is $CWA_{20}q^{-2}$ by \eqref{eq:tail}.
Combining these proves the second bound. Strict $|\delta|>D$ matches
the source's strict $\beta>\sigma$; equality belongs to the good set.
\end{proof}

\section{What remains at the complex arguments}
Define the nonnegative quantity (not assumed small)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moment}
 M_T(s)=\frac1{Tq}\sum_{I^3}w(1+r)^{-20}
 x^2(1+x)^{22}e^{2sx},\qquad x=q\max_j|\delta_{i_j}|,
\end{equation}
and $P_{22}(\phi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq22}\|\partial^\alpha\phi\|_1$.
\begin{lemma}[Reflected argument bound; \texttt{ORDINATE-ARGUMENT-BOUND-001}]
\label{lem:arguments}
Uniformly for the fixed-support class above and $T$ sufficiently large,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:argument-bound}
 |\Ea|\leq C P_{22}(\phi)M_T(s).
\end{equation}
The moment is finite for every fixed $T$. Its convergence to zero has
not been established.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For one triple set $y=(\ell(\delta_{i_2}+\delta_{i_1}),
\ell(\delta_{i_3}+\delta_{i_1}))$ and
$G(t)=J(a,d;t\delta)\{F((u,v)-ity)-F(u,v)\}$ for $-1\leq t\leq1$.
Simultaneous reflection replaces its contribution by
$(G(1)+G(-1))/2$. Since $G(0)=0$, Taylor's integral remainder bounds
this by $\tfrac12\sup_{|t|\leq1}|G''(t)|$.
Inside the Fourier integral the real exponent is $tA$, where
\[
 A=b\{\delta_{i_2}\xi+\delta_{i_3}\eta+
                   \delta_{i_1}(\xi+\eta)\}.
\]
The exact identity $|\xi|+|\eta|+|\xi+\eta|=2h(\xi,\eta)$ gives
$|A|\leq2sx$, since $b/q<1$. Also $|\nabla A|\leq Cx$.
Apply $(1-\Delta_{\xi,\eta})^{10}$ in the Fourier integral.
For $k=0,1,2$, the derivatives of $A^ke^{tA}\phi$ have $L^1$ norm
at most $CP_{22}(\phi)x^k(1+x)^{20}e^{2sx}$.
The integration-by-parts denominator is
$(1+4\pi^2(u^2+v^2))^{10}$, so these estimates supply decay
$(1+r)^{-20}$. Compact support makes the boundary terms zero.
In $G''$, the three product-rule terms involve respectively $J''$,
$2J'$ and $J$, where the first two derivatives along $t\delta$ are
$O((x/q)^2)$ and $O(x/q)$. Bound the undifferentiated difference
of tests by the sum of their absolute values. It follows that
\[
 |G''(t)|\leq CP_{22}(\phi)(1+r)^{-20}
                  x^2(1+x)^{22}e^{2sx}.
\]
Sum this estimate using Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}. Finiteness of
\eqref{eq:moment} follows from $x\leq q/2$ at fixed $T$ and the
global count \eqref{eq:global-count}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Exact remaining criterion.}\label{target:arguments}
For each fixed admissible test and smoothing, \eqref{eq:main-transfer},
the baseline theorem, and Theorem~\ref{thm:real-kernel} imply
\[
 O_T[F]=S_3[F]+o(1)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Ea=o(1).
\]
This is a consequence of \texttt{ORDINATE-TRANSFER-001}, not a new
assertion that either limit holds. In particular $M_T(s)=o(1)$ would
be sufficient. The other named errors are the inherited
$R_T^J-S_3[F]$, the two bounds \eqref{eq:kernel-errors}, and the exact
scale error $(q/b-1)S_3[F]$; all enter with the signs in
\eqref{eq:main-transfer}. For varying tests one must make each of
these bounds, including $P_{22}(\phi)M_T(s)$, tend to zero.

\paragraph{Why the density input does not finish this step.}
Small unscaled $\delta$ does not give small $q\delta$.
The density bound gives no vanishing bound for the fraction at
$|\delta|=c/q$, with fixed $c>0$. The cutoff $64\log q/q$ used for
the kernel leaves shifts of size $O(\log q)$ in the test arguments.
It therefore cannot be used to make those arguments real by continuity.
Signed cancellation might still prove $\Ea=o(1)$ without the positive
moment condition; we assert no impossibility theorem.

\paragraph{Finite sensitivity check (\texttt{SYNTHETIC\_MODEL}).}
Put occurrences with $\delta=\pm c/q$ at one positive ordinate and
include their negative conjugates. At zero vertical gaps the kernel
tends to $J_*$ as $q\to\infty$. Averaging a Fourier mode over the
eight ordered choices of positive-ordinate signs gives the multiplier
\[
 \cosh((b/q)c\xi)\cosh((b/q)c\eta)
                       \cosh((b/q)c(\xi+\eta)).
\]
Its limit is greater than one when $c>0$ and $(\xi,\eta)\ne(0,0)$.
This proves pointwise sensitivity after symmetry averaging. A finite
quartet has vanishing mass after normalization by $Tq$; it is not a
counterexample to the zeta asymptotic. A Fourier mode is used only for
this finite identity, not as an admissible Schwartz test.

\section{Eight reflections and the surviving signed term}
Task: proof. Assumptions remain \texttt{UNCONDITIONAL} and the displayed
Fourier support. In this section $\delta_j=\delta_{i_j}$ is in slot order
(anchor first); write $\mathcal J(\delta)=J(a,d;\delta_2,\delta_3,\delta_1)$.
For $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^3$ and $S\subseteq\{1,2,3\}$ set
\[
 \chi_S(\varepsilon)=\prod_{j\in S}\varepsilon_j,\qquad
 J_S=\frac18\sum_\varepsilon\chi_S(\varepsilon)
                          \mathcal J(\varepsilon\delta).
\]
Thus $J_\varnothing$ is an average, whereas $J_0=\mathcal J(0)$
is a parameter specialization. These are different functions.
Put $a_1=b\delta_1(\xi+\eta)$, $a_2=b\delta_2\xi$, $a_3=b\delta_3\eta$,
$C_j=\cosh a_j$, $S_j=\sinh a_j$, and $P=2\pi(u\xi+v\eta)$.

\begin{lemma}[Eight reflections; \texttt{ORDINATE-SIGN-AVERAGE-001}]
\label{lem:sign-average}
For every tuple and frequency,
\begin{align}
 \frac18\sum_\varepsilon\mathcal J(\varepsilon\delta)
       (e^{\sum_j\varepsilon_ja_j}-1)
 &=J_\varnothing(C_1C_2C_3-1)\notag\\
 &\quad+\sum_{S\ne\varnothing}J_S
           \prod_{j\in S}S_j\prod_{j\notin S}C_j.\label{eq:eight-sign}
\end{align}
Define $E_{\rm even}$ and $E_{\rm kernel\text{-}mix}$ by multiplying the
first and second terms, respectively, by $c_Tw\phi(\xi,\eta)e^{iP}$,
integrating over $\R^2$, and summing over all ordered occurrences. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signed-split}
 \Ea=E_{\rm even}+E_{\rm kernel\text{-}mix}.
\end{equation}
These operations are absolutely convergent at fixed $T$. Uniformly in all
real gaps and $|\delta_j|\leq1/2$,
\begin{align}
 \overline{J_S}&=(-1)^{|S|}J_S,\qquad J_\varnothing>0,\label{eq:sign-parity}\\
 |J_S|&\leq C\prod_{j\in S}|\delta_j|\quad(S\ne\varnothing),\label{eq:sign-size}\\
 |J_S|&\leq C\min(|a|+|d|,1)\prod_{j\in S}|\delta_j|
                         \quad(|S|\text{ odd}).\label{eq:odd-gap}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expand $e^{\varepsilon_ja_j}=C_j+\varepsilon_jS_j$, multiply, and use
orthogonality of the eight characters $\chi_S$. The subtracted constant
has coefficient $J_\varnothing$, proving \eqref{eq:eight-sign}.
Horizontal reflection is a multiplicity-preserving involution of zero
occurrences. Apply it independently in each slot of a finite sum with
cutoffs $|\gamma_{i_j}|\leq U_j$. These cutoffs and the anchor weight are
invariant. An individual index-diagonal class need not be invariant;
we reindex the whole Cartesian product, never a separated diagonal class.

For completeness the Fourier interchange needs a kernel majorant even
before using Schwartz decay. With $k(t)=(1+t^2)^{-1}$, the integral profile
is bounded by $C\int k(t)k(t-a)k(t-d)\dd t$. Unit counts give
$\sum_i k(v-\gamma_i)\ll\log(|v|+2)$: split into unit intervals near
$v$ and sum $\log(|v|+|n|+2)/(1+n^2)$.
For a fixed anchor, the sum of this profile majorant over both partners
is therefore at most
$C\int k(v-\gamma_{i_1})\log^2(|v|+2)\dd v<\infty$.
There are finitely many anchors. On the compact Fourier support every
exponential is bounded by $e^{2sx}\leq T^s$ at fixed $T$.
This supplies absolute Fubini and independent cutoff limits for all eight
terms, and proves \eqref{eq:signed-split}.

Conjugating the profile reflects all three horizontal parameters, which
proves the first identity in \eqref{eq:sign-parity}. In its integral,
independent sign averaging replaces each scalar kernel by
\[
 \frac12\left(\frac1{1+(t-ie)^2}+\frac1{1+(t+ie)^2}\right)
 =\frac{1+t^2-e^2}{(1+t^2-e^2)^2+4t^2e^2}>0.
\]
Use this with the three real centers $a,d,0$ to obtain $J_\varnothing>0$.
For any fixed derivative order, real-center and horizontal derivatives
of a scalar kernel are $O((1+t^2)^{-1})$ on the closed strip. The profile
therefore has uniformly bounded mixed derivatives of the orders used here,
by the same domination as Lemma~\ref{lem:transfer}.
An odd finite difference in each coordinate in $S$, followed by the
fundamental theorem of calculus in those coordinates, gives
\eqref{eq:sign-size}. Also
$\mathcal J(-a,-d;\delta)=\mathcal J(a,d;-\delta)$ by $t\mapsto-t$.
At zero gaps all odd-cardinality coefficients vanish identically in
$\delta$. Apply the real-gap mean value theorem to the preceding mixed
finite-difference integral, and combine with \eqref{eq:sign-size}, to
obtain \eqref{eq:odd-gap}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Complete quadratic term; \texttt{ORDINATE-QUADRATIC-001}]
\label{lem:quadratic}
Let $D_1=\partial_u+\partial_v$, $D_2=\partial_u$, $D_3=\partial_v$,
and $K_j=\partial_{\delta_j}\mathcal J(0)$. The sign-averaged contribution
before multiplying by $c_Tw$ has the expansion
\begin{align}
 &\frac18\sum_\varepsilon\mathcal J(\varepsilon\delta)
       \{F((u,v)-iy(\varepsilon\delta))-F(u,v)\}
       =Q_\delta[F]+\mathcal R_\delta[F],\label{eq:quadratic-expansion}\\
 &Q_\delta[F]= -\frac{J_0\ell^2}{2}\sum_{j=1}^3\delta_j^2D_j^2F
                 -i\ell\sum_{j=1}^3\delta_j^2K_jD_jF,\label{eq:quadratic}\\
 &|\mathcal R_\delta[F]|\leq C P_{24}(\phi)x^4(1+r)^{-20}
                         \quad(0\leq x\leq1).\label{eq:quadratic-remainder}
\end{align}
Here $y(\delta)=\ell(\delta_1+\delta_2,\delta_1+\delta_3)$,
$P_{24}(\phi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq24}\|\partial^\alpha\phi\|_1$,
and derivatives of $F$ are evaluated on the real plane. The $K_j$ are
purely imaginary and $|K_j|\ll\min(|a|+|d|,1)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Along $t\delta$ the first and second test derivatives at zero are
\[
-i\ell\sum_j\delta_jD_jF,\qquad
-\ell^2\sum_{j,k}\delta_j\delta_kD_jD_kF.
\]
In the product with $\mathcal J(t\delta)$, the constant test difference
is zero. The quadratic coefficient is consequently the half second test
derivative times $J_0$, plus the product of the first derivatives.
Independent sign averaging kills $\delta_j\delta_k$ for $j\ne k$,
giving both terms in \eqref{eq:quadratic}. Conjugation and the zero-gap
identity in Lemma~\ref{lem:sign-average} prove the assertions about $K_j$.

The averaged function of $t$ is even. Taylor's theorem to degree three
leaves a remainder bounded by its fourth derivative divided by $24$.
The profile derivatives of order $m$ along $t\delta$ are $O((x/q)^m)$.
Fourier integration by parts, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:arguments}, bounds
its accompanying test derivative of order $4-m$ by
$CP_{24}x^{4-m}(1+r)^{-20}$ on $x\leq1$, $|t|\leq1$.
For $m=4$, bound the test difference by the two tests. This proves
\eqref{eq:quadratic-remainder}. No Taylor expansion on $x>1$ is asserted.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Signed error budget; \texttt{ORDINATE-CANCELLATION-BUDGET-001}]
\label{thm:cancellation-budget}
For $0<s=1-\kappa<1$, define
\[
 M_{24,T}(s)=\frac1{Tq}\sum w(1+r)^{-20}
                         x^2(1+x)^{24}e^{2sx}.
\]
For sufficiently large $T$, with absolute constants,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signed-budget}
 |E_{\rm even}|\leq CP_{24}M_{24,T}(s),\qquad
 |E_{\rm kernel\text{-}mix}|\leq\frac{CP_{24}}{q^2}M_{24,T}(s).
\end{equation}
These bounds hold also for sums restricted to any reflection-invariant
set of tuples, with the restricted moment on the right. In particular,
for fixed $m>0$ the restrictions $x\leq m$ satisfy
\[
 |E_{\rm even}^{\leq m}|\ll_m WP_{24}q,\qquad
 |E_{\rm kernel\text{-}mix}^{\leq m}|\ll_m WP_{24}/q.
\]
For $0<\epsilon\leq1$, the summed remainder from
\eqref{eq:quadratic-remainder} on $x\leq\epsilon$ is at most
$CWP_{24}q\epsilon^4$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply $(1-\Delta_{\xi,\eta})^{11}$ to the Fourier integrands; its
integration-by-parts denominator gives $(1+r)^{-22}$ decay.
The $L^1$ norms of the resulting derivatives of
$\phi(\prod C_j-1)$ are bounded by
$CP_{24}x^2(1+x)^{22}e^{2sx}$. This follows either by twice differentiating
in the common scale of the $a_j$, or by the quadratic vanishing of the
product of coshes at zero. Derivatives of
$\phi\prod_{j\in S}S_j\prod_{j\notin S}C_j$ have $L^1$ norm at most
$CP_{24}x^{|S|}(1+x)^{22}e^{2sx}$: a differentiated sinh retains a
factor from $\nabla a_j$, and an undifferentiated one is bounded by
$C x e^{|a_j|}$. All exponents together are at most $2sx$.

Write $m=|S|$. The profile bounds multiply these estimates by
$C(x/q)^m$, and additionally by $\min(|a|+|d|,1)$ when $m$ is odd.
Since $\ell\asymp q$, this extra factor is at most $Cr/q$.
For $m=1,2,3$, the products are bounded respectively by
\[
 C\frac{x^2r}{q^2},\qquad C\frac{x^4}{q^2},\qquad
 C\frac{x^6r}{q^4}\leq C\frac{x^4r}{q^2},
\]
using $x/q\leq1/2$ in the last step. The extra $r$ is absorbed by the
22 powers of real-plane decay; the extra $x^2$ by the 24 powers in the
moment. This proves \eqref{eq:signed-budget} pointwise after Fourier
integration and hence after any indicated restriction and summation.
The counting argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:counts}, applied directly to
$(1+r)^{-20}$, gives $(Tq)^{-1}\sum w(1+r)^{-20}\ll Wq$.
It proves the restricted statements and the summed Taylor remainder.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Density bound for kernel mixing; \texttt{ORDINATE-DENSITY-MIX-001}]
\label{thm:density-mix}
Put $\alpha=331/4000$. For fixed $0<s<1/8$, uniformly for tests supported
in $h\leq s$, as $T\to\infty$,
\begin{align}
 M_{24,T}(s)\ll_s W\{&q^{1+8s}(1+\log q)^{26}
                +q^{28}T^{s-\alpha}+q^{29}T^{s-1}\},\label{eq:density-moment}\\
 |E_{\rm kernel\text{-}mix}|\ll_s WP_{24}\{&q^{-1+8s}(1+\log q)^{26}
                +q^{26}T^{s-\alpha}+q^{27}T^{s-1}\}.\label{eq:density-mix}
\end{align}
Thus $E_{\rm kernel\text{-}mix}=o(1)$ for fixed test and smoothing when
$0<s<\alpha$. In this smaller support region the ordinate-only target is
therefore equivalent to $E_{\rm even}=o(1)$, which remains open.
The endpoint $s=\alpha$ is not included.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Set $R_*=T\ell/2$. On $r\leq R_*$ all three ordinates belong to
$[T/2,5T/2]$. For $0\leq v\leq(331/1000)q$, reflection and the density
input on dyadic windows from $T/4$ through $4T$ bound the number of
occurrences there with $|\delta|>v/q$ by
\[
 B_T(v)\ll Tq e^{-v/4}+q^2.
\]
Replacing a dyadic window's logarithm by $q$ in the exponent costs an
absolute factor because $v/q\leq331/1000$. At large $T$ every window
satisfies the source height threshold. On a shell $r\leq R$, a fixed bad
occurrence, in any of the three slots, has at most
$Cq^2(1+R/\ell)^2$ possible partners, by unit counts within distance
$2R/\ell$. Dyadic summation against $(1+r)^{-20}$ therefore gives
\[
 \frac1{Tq}\sum_{\substack{r\leq R_*\\x>v}}w(1+r)^{-20}
       \ll W(q^2e^{-v/4}+q^3/T).
\]
It is also at most $CWq$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:counts}. On $r>R_*$ the
global count gives weighted mass at most $CWq^2R_*^{-16}$.
These estimates include all slots, multiplicities and all gap sizes;
no density theorem is applied to arbitrarily remote partner heights.

Let $g(v)=v^2(1+v)^{24}e^{2sv}$, an increasing function with $g(0)=0$.
Tonelli gives the exact layer integral of $g'(v)$ against the preceding
weighted tail count, up to $v=q/2$. For $v\leq v_0=4\log q$, use
$CWq$; the contribution is at most $CWqg(v_0)$.
For $v_0<v\leq(331/1000)q$, use the density estimate. Since
$1/4-2s>0$, its exponential part contributes at most
\[
 CWq^2\int_{v_0}^\infty(1+v)^{26}e^{-(1/4-2s)v}\dd v
 \ll_s Wq^{1+8s}(1+\log q)^{26}.
\]
Above $(331/1000)q$, use monotonicity of the tail count at that endpoint;
its exponential part is at most $CWq^2T^{-\alpha}g(q/2)$.
The additive density remainder contributes $CW(q^3/T)g(q/2)$.
Finally the remote-gap contribution is at most
$CWq^2R_*^{-16}g(q/2)$. Since $g(q/2)\ll q^{26}T^s$, these are the
last two terms of \eqref{eq:density-moment}, with the remote-gap term
absorbed into the last one. The split requires only sufficiently large
$T$ so that $v_0<(331/1000)q$.
Apply \eqref{eq:signed-budget} to finish. For $s<\alpha<1/8$ all three
terms of \eqref{eq:density-mix} vanish. Constants may depend on $s$;
no uniform limit at $s=1/8$ or $s=\alpha$ is asserted.
\end{proof}

\section{Constant-kernel replacement in the even error}
Task: proof and bookkeeping. Assumptions: \texttt{UNCONDITIONAL} and
\texttt{SUPPORT}\,$(h\leq s,\ 0<s<1)$ for the general bound; the
vanishing conclusion requires the smaller fixed range stated below.
Define the tuple integral
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-tuple}
 \mathcal H_\delta(u,v)=\int_{\R^2}\phi(\xi,\eta)
 e^{2\pi i(u\xi+v\eta)}
 \left(\prod_{j=1}^3\cosh a_j-1\right)\dd\xi\dd\eta,
\end{equation}
with $a_j$ as in the eight-reflection lemma. In the following definitions
the Fourier integral is evaluated for each tuple before summation:
\begin{align}
 E_{\rm constant}&=c_TJ_*\sum_{I^3}w\mathcal H_\delta(u,v),\notag\\
 E_{\rm replacement}&=c_T\sum_{I^3}w(J_\varnothing-J_*)
                                      \mathcal H_\delta(u,v).
                                      \label{eq:constant-errors}
\end{align}

\begin{lemma}[\texttt{ORDINATE-CONSTANT-KERNEL-001}]
\label{lem:constant-kernel}
For all real gaps and $|\delta_j|\leq1/2$, with an absolute constant,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-kernel-bound}
 |J_\varnothing-J_*|
 \leq C\min\left(a^2+d^2+\sum_{j=1}^3\delta_j^2,1\right)
 \leq \frac{C(r^2+x^2)}{q^2}
\end{equation}
where the last inequality holds for sufficiently large $T$ in the project
scaling. At fixed $T\geq2\pi e$, both sums in \eqref{eq:constant-errors}
converge absolutely, and exactly
\begin{align}
 E_{\rm even}&=E_{\rm constant}+E_{\rm replacement},
                                                   \label{eq:constant-split}\\
 E_{\rm constant}&=\frac{2\pi}{Tq}\sum_{I^3}w
                   \{F(z_{21},z_{31})-F(u,v)\}.\label{eq:constant-zero-sum}
\end{align}
For sufficiently large $T$ and $0<s<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-moment-bound}
 |E_{\rm replacement}|\leq \frac{CP_{24}(\phi)}{q^2}M_{24,T}(s).
\end{equation}
In particular, writing $\alpha=331/4000$, for each fixed $0<s<1/8$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-density-bound}
 |E_{\rm replacement}|\ll_s WP_{24}(\phi)
 \left\{q^{-1+8s}(1+\log q)^{26}
       +q^{26}T^{s-\alpha}+q^{27}T^{s-1}\right\}.
\end{equation}
It is $o(1)$ for fixed test and smoothing on $h\leq s<\alpha$,
with $s>0$. The endpoint $s=\alpha$ is excluded.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Independent sign averaging makes $J_\varnothing$ even in each horizontal
coordinate. Uniform second horizontal derivatives, established in
Lemma~\ref{lem:sign-average}, and Taylor's integral remainder along the
segment from $0$ to $\delta$ give
$|J_\varnothing-J_0|\leq C\sum_j\delta_j^2$, uniformly in the real gaps.
The real-gap estimate in Theorem~\ref{thm:real-kernel} is
$|J_0-J_*|\leq C\min(a^2+d^2,1)$.
Combine these bounds with global boundedness of the kernels to obtain
the first inequality in \eqref{eq:constant-kernel-bound}.
For the second use $|a|,|d|\leq r/\ell$, $|\delta_j|\leq x/q$,
and $\ell\asymp q$ for sufficiently large $T$. Coincident centers and
the closed horizontal endpoints obey the same bounds.

The integration-by-parts estimate from the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:cancellation-budget}, before discarding two powers of
real-plane decay, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constant-tuple-decay}
 |\mathcal H_\delta(u,v)|\leq
 CP_{24}(\phi)x^2(1+x)^{22}e^{2sx}(1+r)^{-22}.
\end{equation}
This estimate is valid at fixed $T\geq2\pi e$ as well: it uses only
$b/q<1$, the compact support and derivatives of the Fourier amplitude.
At fixed $T$, $x\leq q/2$, and unit zero counts show that
$\sum w(1+r)^{-22}<\infty$. Boundedness of $J_\varnothing$ proves
absolute convergence of both integrated sums. Their summand identity
proves \eqref{eq:constant-split}.

For the estimate, multiply \eqref{eq:constant-tuple-decay} by the second
bound in \eqref{eq:constant-kernel-bound} and use
\[
 (r^2+x^2)(1+x)^{22}(1+r)^{-22}
 \leq (1+x)^{24}(1+r)^{-20}.
\]
The normalization $c_T=16/(3Tq)$ now gives
\eqref{eq:constant-moment-bound}. Apply
Theorem~\ref{thm:density-mix} to obtain \eqref{eq:constant-density-bound}
and its strict-support vanishing conclusion.

Finally the finite expansion of the product of coshes identifies
$\mathcal H_\delta$ exactly with
\[
 \frac18\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^3}
 \{F((u,v)-iy(\varepsilon\delta))-F(u,v)\}.
\]
Each of these eight test differences is absolutely summable at fixed $T$
by compact-Fourier integration by parts in the bounded imaginary strip.
Reindex each slot of the full ordered sum by horizontal reflection,
first with independent cutoffs $|\gamma_{i_j}|\leq U_j$ and then by
absolute convergence. Since $c_TJ_*=2\pi/(Tq)$, this proves
\eqref{eq:constant-zero-sum}. The reindexing retains all occurrences and
all diagonal patterns collectively; it does not restrict them separately.
No absolute interchange of the unweighted infinite zero sum with the
unevaluated Fourier integral is asserted. Remove the cutoffs at fixed
$T$, and only then take the height limit with all test data fixed.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Remaining cancellation, stated precisely.}
For the original support class $h\leq1-\kappa$, the open estimate is
$E_{\rm constant}+E_{\rm replacement}+E_{\rm kernel\text{-}mix}=o(1)$.
For each fixed $0<s<331/4000$, the transfer identity, the two real-test
kernel estimates and the two vanishing errors just proved give
\[
 O_T[F]=S_3[F]+o(1)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 E_{\rm constant}=\frac{2\pi}{Tq}\sum w
       \{F(z_{21},z_{31})-F(u,v)\}=o(1).
\]
This equivalence is a consequence of the transfer identity and
Lemma~\ref{lem:constant-kernel}; the vanishing assertion on the right
is not proved. The bound \eqref{eq:density-moment} does not make it
vanish: even its first upper-bound term grows with $q$. Positivity of
the constant kernel and of the cosh difference does not imply positivity
of the oscillatory, test-paired expression. Reflection cancels neither
its quadratic part nor its full even part. A new signed weighted
correlation estimate, or stronger microscopic horizontal information,
is needed for this route. This is not an impossibility theorem.

\section{Provenance and scope}
This extension uses \texttt{PAIR-COUNT-001}, the recorded kernel profile,
and the baseline weighted theorem. The two new source statements are
imported as unconditional inputs with their ranges intact. Neither
PCC, RH, simplicity nor an all-zero critical-line specialization occurs
in the reduction. The prior line-by-line audit and archived reproduction
cover the weighted baseline; they do not certify this extension.
The new exact regression script checks finite algebra and normalization,
not asymptotic analysis or the imported theorems.
All limits here fix $\omega,\phi,\kappa$ first, remove zero cutoffs by
absolute convergence at fixed $T$, and then send $T\to\infty$.
Internal Fourier axes and the origin are included; the outer support
boundary is excluded by $\kappa>0$. No new endpoint extension is used.
